Emissions
There are two emissions metrics available projected until 2050:
  • EVIC Intensity, which is the ratio of absolute emissions to Enterprise Value including Cash (EVIC).
  • Revenue Intensity, which is the ratio of absolute emissions to revenue.
  • Intensity $/GDP, which is only available to Sovereign Issuers. Represents the GHG intensity of GDP in units of Kg GHG emissions produced per dollar of GDP.
  • Intensity $/GDP PPP, which is only available to Sovereign Issuers. Represents the GHG intensity of GDP in units of Kg GHG emissions produced per dollar of GDP adjusted by Purchasing Power Parity.
  • Intensity Per Capita, which is only available to Sovereign Issuers. Represents the GHG intensity per capita; average tons of GHG emissions per person.
  • Intensity $/Sov Debt, which is only available to Sovereign Issuers. Represents the GHG intensity of sovereign debt in units of Kg GHG emissions produced per dollar of gross sovereign debt.
  • Financed emissions, which are calculated by taking the absolute emissions and multiplying them by the market value divided by the enterprise value including cash.

Climate Scenario and Issuer Emission Targets
There are three scenarios available for quantifying decarbonization and temperature alignment for corporate issuers:
  • NGFS Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs): Slow technology change with small regional policies updates to those currently implemented based on NDCs,
    resulting in high physical risks with global temperature rise approximately 2.6°C by 2100.
  • NGFS Current Policies: No additional policies beyond those are currently implemented, along with slow technology change, resulting in high physical risks
    with global temperature rise of 3.2°C by 2100.
  • Blended Scenario: This scenario blends 3 NGFS scenarios (NDCs, Current Policies, and Below 2 Degrees). The primary determining factor for this approach is
    the cost of abatement. While this scenario mainly aligns with the Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC), it incorporates the Below 2 Degree decarbonization
    rate for the power sector and automotives. In this scenario, these two sectors are expected to decarbonize at a faster rate due to the relative affordability of
    their abatement processes. Conversely, for industry related emissions in emerging markets (excluding China), we apply the Current Policy decarbonization rate.
    Slower decarbonization reflects the combination of higher costs of abatement and the constrained fiscal capacity of emerging market economies.
Temperature outcomes can be evaluated with emission reduction targets applied, not applied, and with credibility adjustment. This ability allows users to assess the
relative climate performance of an issuer. Issuer targets are sourced from Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi), Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP) and company
reports. Emissions commitments are assessed using the Credibility Framework described by GFANZ, to the extent feasible. The credibility weighting is applied when
projecting firm emissions. A lower credibility score results in a reduced weighting of the commitment when projecting corporate decarbonization.
